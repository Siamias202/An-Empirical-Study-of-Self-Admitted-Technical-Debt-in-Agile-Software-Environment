RQ2 answer

The observable-history comparison included 3,662 issues (468 SATD) from 3 projects. Overall-duration comparisons included 202,213 issues from 25 projects.

Scope: the largest count difference was scope_change_count (SATD median 1.00, non-SATD median 0.00; Cliff's delta +0.145, negligible; Holm p <0.001), indicating more observed activity for SATD issues.
Estimation: the largest count difference was estimation_change_count (SATD median 0.00, non-SATD median 0.00; Cliff's delta +0.057, negligible; Holm p 0.025), indicating more observed activity for SATD issues.
Ownership: the largest count difference was ownership_change_count (SATD median 2.00, non-SATD median 1.00; Cliff's delta +0.125, negligible; Holm p <0.001), indicating more observed activity for SATD issues.
Workflow: the largest count difference was status_transition_count (SATD median 2.00, non-SATD median 2.00; Cliff's delta +0.057, negligible; Holm p 0.335), indicating more observed activity for SATD issues.
Temporal evolution: SATD issues had a substantially longer observed lifespan (median 728.2 versus 129.9 days; Cliff's delta +0.246, small; Holm p <0.001).
No positive stage-duration difference remained significant after Holm correction.
The largest supported issue-level transition difference was DEVELOPMENT->REVIEW (19.0% of SATD versus 11.5% of non-SATD issues; difference +7.5 percentage points).

Overall, several comparisons may be statistically detectable, but count-effect magnitudes are generally negligible, providing limited evidence of substantially different process trajectories.
The strongest broad difference is temporal: SATD-labelled issues remain observable much longer and, among issues with recorded effort, tend to accumulate more effort. This is an association, not evidence that longer duration or instability causes SATD.
Because detailed histories cover only three projects and only issues with at least one recorded transition, the process and transition findings are exploratory and do not support a dataset-wide causal claim.
